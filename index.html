    How it works

    1. Listen. Your song is decoded and run through an FFT. For a low, a mid and a high frequency band it measures where new energy appears. Autocorrelation and a comb-filter search lock the tempo and beat grid, and a small neural network works out which beat is beat 1.
    2. Think. A 1.7M-parameter transformer writes the drum part one 16th note at a time. At each step it sees the song's onset pattern around that moment, the drums it has already played, and where it is in the bar and phrase. It decides kick, snare, clap, hats, toms, crash and percussion together, with ghost notes and accents.
    3. Play. Every drum is synthesised live with the Web Audio API; there are no samples. Edit anything, then save or export.

    Prefer the original hand-written probability tables? Switch Drummer to Classic rules. Both engines run entirely in your browser.

    About the AI drummer

    The drummer is a small causal transformer (4 layers, 1.7M parameters) trained from scratch on about 9,500 real songs, roughly a million bars of human drumming with their accompaniment, taken from the Lakh MIDI dataset. Its twelve styles were not written by hand: they are groups the model found by clustering the drum patterns of those songs.

    To make it work on real audio rather than clean MIDI, each training excerpt was also rendered to audio and analysed by this page's own analyser, so the network learned from exactly the features it will be given when you upload a song.

    How good is it?

    Held-out test songs by artists the model never saw. Each system hears only the song and tries to reproduce the part its real drummer played (F1 score on drum hits, 1.0 = identical). A fixed rock beat is included as a sanity baseline: real drummers play a lot of rock, so it scores well.
